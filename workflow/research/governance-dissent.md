# Preserving dissent without contagion — prior art and what thebus should borrow

Date: 2026-08-18
Scope: just-in-time research for thebus, the governance layer for LLM agent fleets.
Question: how do human and machine systems keep dissent in the record without letting it set every
future reader's priors, and which of their mechanisms transfer?

Method: five parallel primary-source sweeps (formal procedure, high-stakes organizations, on-chain
governance, social-epistemic literature, minority-cluster tooling) plus direct reading of the
psycho-legal and minority-influence literatures. Every claim below carries its URL.

---

## What I found

### 0. The one-paragraph answer

Every mature system preserves dissent and denies it **operative force**. Not one of them hides it
from newcomers. Robert's Rules receives a minority report but forbids acting on it without a motion
to substitute; a judicial dissent publishes on the same page as the majority and creates no binding
precedent; Quakers minute "two Friends were not in unity"; sociocracy logs a non-qualifying concern
against a dated review; corporate law says an unrecorded dissent legally never happened. The axis
these systems cut on is **non-operative versus non-visible**, and they all choose non-operative.
thebus's DISSENT REGISTER conflates the two: it takes the universally-correct move (strip authority)
and bolts on a move nothing in the corpus does (strip visibility at orientation). That is the single
biggest divergence from prior art, and section 6 argues it is a defect.

The second structural finding is that **the filing mechanism is never what fails**. Across NASA,
State, Toyota, aviation, and the IDF, the copyable part — a form, a channel, a register — is trivial
and universally present. What separates the systems that work from the ones that produce
catastrophes is a **named responder, a clock, and staffing**. GM copied the andon cord and not the
1:6 team-leader ratio; NASA has a formal dissent process with no day-count; the IDF's devil's
advocate had a bypass routing line and no mandatory-response clock, and its September 2023 warnings
went unanswered.

### 1. Formal governance procedure

**A second is a throughput gate, not an endorsement.** Robert's stated rationale is time economy:
"Every motion should be seconded. This is to prevent time being consumed in considering a question
that only one person favors." An opponent may second so the motion can be voted down.
<https://www.constitution.org/1-Constitution/rror/rror-01.htm>

**Minority reports are received for information and cannot be adopted.** After the committee report
is read, "it is usual to allow the minority to present their views," but reception requires a
majority vote and the views are informational — to have operative effect "any one can move to
substitute the resolutions they recommend for those recommended by the committee."
<https://www.constitution.org/1-Constitution/rror/rror-09.htm>

**Reconsideration requires a defector from the winning side.** "It must be made by one who voted
with the prevailing side," and only on the day of the vote or the next. Being unhappy with the
outcome is not a ground. The rule is a costly-signal test: reopening requires someone who *won* to
publicly say they were wrong. <https://www.constitution.org/1-Constitution/rror/rror-06.htm>
<https://mrsc.org/stay-informed/mrsc-insight/july-2025/changing-course>

**Renewal is barred within a session.** An adopted or rejected main motion "cannot be again brought
before the assembly at the same session, except by a motion to reconsider or to rescind." Motions
that do renew freely may only do so "provided there has been progress in debate or business" —
renewal requires that something actually changed.
<https://www.constitution.org/1-Constitution/rror/rror-06.htm>

**Repetition is capped and queued.** No member speaks more than twice a day to the same question,
and "no member can speak a second time to a question as long as any member desires to speak who has
not spoken to the question." <https://www.constitution.org/1-Constitution/rror/rror-07.htm>
The chair may refuse recognition to members "using parliamentary forms merely to obstruct business."
<https://www.constitution.org/1-Constitution/rror/rror-06.htm>

**Silencing costs a supermajority.** Previous Question and Limit Debate require two-thirds, on the
principle that parliamentary law requires "a two-thirds vote for every motion that suppresses a main
question for the session without free debate." Rules cannot be suspended "when the negative vote is
as large as the minority protected by that rule."
<https://www.constitution.org/1-Constitution/rror/rror-05.htm>
<https://www.constitution.org/1-Constitution/rror/rror-04.htm>

**Minutes record what is done, not what is said** — so dissent enters the permanent record only by
being converted into a formal artifact. <https://www.constitution.org/1-Constitution/rror/rror-10.htm>
US House Rule XI cl. 2(l) gives a noticing member "not less than two additional calendar days" to
file written minority views: a guaranteed slot with a deadline, so dissent cannot stall the report.
<https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CPRT-110HPRT32742/html/CPRT-110HPRT32742.htm>

**Sociocracy/Holacracy: the objection must pass a harm test, and the group judges it.** S3
distinguishes an Objection (an argument revealing risk) from a Concern ("an assumption that cannot,
for now at least, be backed up by reasoning or enough evidence") — concerns are logged, not honored.
<https://patterns.sociocracy30.org/consent-decision-making.html> The circle, not the objector,
rules on qualification. <https://patterns.sociocracy30.org/test-arguments-qualify-as-objections.html>

Holacracy Constitution v5.0 §5.3.2 requires a reasonable argument for **all four** of: the proposal
would reduce the circle's capacity to enact its purpose; it would limit the objector's own role
capacity; the concern does not already exist absent the proposal; and the impact would be either
necessary or unadaptable before significant harm. The Facilitator "may only judge whether the
Objector presented arguments for each criteria using logical reasoning" and "may not judge on the
basis of an argument's accuracy" — and "if it fails to meet the required criteria, the Facilitator
must discard it." <https://www.holacracy.org/constitution/5-0/>

v4.1 §3.2.4(c) adds the data-versus-prediction rule: the tension must be "triggered just by presently
known facts or events, without regard to a prediction of what might happen in the future. However,
relying on predictions is allowed when no opportunity to adequately sense and respond is likely to
exist in the future before significant impact could result."
<https://www.holacracy.org/constitution/4-1/>

**Objecting obligates repair.** §5.3.4: "The Objector must attempt to find an amendment to the
Proposal that will resolve the Objection and still address the Proposer's Tension. If the Facilitator
believes the Objector is not making a good faith attempt to do so, the Facilitator must deem the
Objection abandoned and drop it." Resolution requires both parties to confirm.
<https://www.holacracy.org/constitution/5-0/>

**"Good enough for now, safe enough to try"** removes "I'd prefer a better option" from the space of
valid objections: the test is reduced capacity, not unimproved capacity.
<https://patterns.sociocracy30.org/consent-decision-making.html> Unresolved dissent is absorbed by a
mandatory **review date** carrying "review date, relevant metrics, and how they will be monitored"
and "information about any concerns raised."
<https://patterns.sociocracy30.org/evaluate-and-evolve-policies.html>

**Quakers have the most explicit anti-repetition rule found anywhere.** Quaker faith & practice 3.10:
"A pause after each contribution will enable what has been said to find its right place in the mind
of the meeting. **Do not repeat views which you have already expressed.**"
<https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/passage/3-10/> And 3.11 closes the wordsmithing loophole: "Do not, under
the pretext of altering the minute, raise new matter for discussion or reiterate your original
contribution." <https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/passage/3-11/>

Silence is explicitly *not* consent — the clerk must re-canvass: "leave time and opportunity for
those who have previously disagreed to indicate whether they are ready to unite with the minute."
<https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/passage/3-16/> Blocking is not a unilateral right: "no one, after
prayerful consideration by the meeting, can 'stand in the way of a decision' without the meeting's
permission." <https://www.friendsjournal.org/2010090/> Baltimore Yearly Meeting states the two-sided
test — "failure to act is also a form of judgment" balanced against "the many occasions when the
leading of one or a few has proven to be the way of Truth."
<https://www.bym-rsf.org/publications/fandp/1988approved/88section4.html>

**Threshing meetings are a venue that decides nothing**: "No decisions are made at threshing
meetings," facilitated so everyone gets a turn.
<https://www.fgcquaker.org/exercises/threshing/> <https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/passage/3-26/>

**Consensus practice grades dissent.** Reservations recorded (decision proceeds, concern logged);
stand aside (won't help implement, won't block) — and in Butler's Formal Consensus "this unresolved
concern is then written down with the proposal"; block, which "must be based on a generally
recognized principle, not personal preference," with authority split: "the group decides whether a
concern is legitimate; the individual decides whether to block or stand aside."
<https://www.seedsforchange.org.uk/consensus>
<https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/c-t-butler-and-amy-rothstein-on-conflict-and-consensus-a-handbook-on-formal-consensus-decisionm>
Schaub's de facto test is a two-person rule: "Can you convince at least one other person in the group
that the block is legitimate?" <https://effectivecollective.net/library/blocking/> Documented
structural limits include requiring two or more blockers, requiring the blocker to supply an
alternative, and N Street Cohousing's rule invalidating a block if the blocker won't meet repeatedly
with the proposal's authors, after which 75% may proceed.
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_decision-making>
<https://effectivecollective.net/library/inappropriate_blocks>

**IETF RFC 7282 is the sharpest statement of the counter-principle.** "Rough consensus is achieved
when all issues are addressed, but not necessarily accommodated." Hums are not votes. Five against a
hundred can still hold rough consensus if the hundred never engaged the substance: "If there is a
minority of folks who have a valid technical objection, that objection must be dealt with before
consensus can be declared." <https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7282.html>

**Wikipedia solved the amnesiac-rereader problem directly.** WP:PEREN preserves rejected proposals
*bound to the recorded rebuttals*, and re-proposers "should address rebuttals raised in the past."
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Perennial_proposals>
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Consensus>
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Tendentious_editing>

### 2. Institutionalized dissent in high-stakes organizations

**CAIB's central finding is that NASA inverted the burden of proof.** Vol. I Ch. 7, verbatim: "the
Debris Assessment Team was put in the untenable position of having to prove that a safety-of-flight
issue existed without the very images that would permit such a determination… operations must be
proved safe, rather than the other way around. **NASA inverted this burden of proof.**"
<https://govinfo.library.unt.edu/caib/news/report/pdf/vol1/chapters/chapter7.pdf>
Ch. 8 makes it a two-accident pattern: "instead of having to prove it was safe to fly, they were
asked to prove that it was unsafe to fly." Boisjoly on Challenger: "I had no data to quantify it.
But I did say I knew it was away from goodness in the current data base."
<https://govinfo.library.unt.edu/caib/news/report/pdf/vol1/chapters/chapter8.pdf>

Ch. 6 documents three imagery requests and "at least eight missed opportunities," and the circular
trap: analysts were "wanting images to more accurately assess damage while simultaneously needing to
prove to Program managers, as a result of their assessment, that there was a need for images in the
first place." Managers demanded a "mandatory need" that nobody could define — "Most team members,
when asked by the Board what 'mandatory need' meant, replied with a shrug of their shoulders."
<https://govinfo.library.unt.edu/caib/news/report/pdf/vol1/chapters/chapter6.pdf>
Ch. 7 again: "Imagine the difference if any Shuttle manager had simply asked, 'Prove to me that
Columbia has not been harmed.'" The Board benchmarked Naval Reactors, which practices "Insistence on
airing minority opinions" and where "when no minority opinions are present, the responsibility for a
thorough and critical examination falls to management."
Ch. 8: "Signals were overlooked, people were silenced, and useful information and dissenting views on
technical issues did not surface at higher levels."
CAIB R7.5-1 demands an independent Technical Engineering Authority, "funded directly from NASA
Headquarters," with "no connection to or responsibility for schedule or program cost."
<https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/caib-report.pdf>

**What NASA built.** NPR 7120.5F §3.3 splits Programmatic Authority from Institutional/Technical
Authority as "a system of checks and balances to provide independent oversight," with TAs "funded
independent of the Programmatic Authority" and formal TA concurrence required on technical decisions.
<https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7120_005F_&page_name=Chapter3>
NESC was established 15 July 2003 out of CAIB, program-independent, reporting to the Chief Engineer.
<https://spacenews.com/nasa-announces-independent-engineering-and-safety-center/>
<https://www.nasa.gov/nesc/about/>

NPR 7120.5F §3.4 Formal Dissent: any team member may trigger it, with no rank threshold; escalation
runs "to management at successively higher levels of both Programmatic Authority and Technical
Authority until resolution," with "final appeals… to the NASA Administrator"; parties jointly document
"the issue, including agreed-to facts, discussion of the differing positions with rationale and
impacts, and the parties' recommendations," and "management's decision on the dissent memorandum…
becomes part of the program or project record"; the policy states "no suppression or retribution."
**It specifies no day-count** — resolution "occurs prior to Implementation whenever possible."
<https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7120_005F_&page_name=Chapter3>
<https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7120_011A_&page_name=Chapter5>
<https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/OCE_docs/OCE_45.pdf>

**The State Department Dissent Channel has the strongest clock in the survey.** 2 FAM 070, created
1971 by Secretary Rogers. Any US-citizen employee of State or USAID may file on substantive policy;
Policy Planning must **acknowledge within 2 working days** and give a **substantive reply normally
within 60 working days** (CRS puts the range at 30–60); distribution routes outside the chain of
command to the Secretary, Deputy Secretary and Under Secretary for Political Affairs; chiefs of
mission must promptly authorize transmission. Non-retaliation is backed by penalty: employees "found
to have engaged in retaliation or reprisal against Dissent Channel users, or to have divulged to
unauthorized personnel the source or contents of Dissent Channel messages, will be subject to
disciplinary action." <https://fam.state.gov/fam/02fam/02fam0070.html>
<https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF10631.html>

AFSA pays for dissent: four grade-stratified Constructive Dissent Awards (Harriman, Rivkin, Herter,
Tex Harris), $4,000 and a trophy each — and nominees "may or may not have used the formal dissent
channel." <https://afsa.org/constructive-dissent-awards>

The honest verdict on it: CRS finds few dissent messages appear impactful and most administrations
ignore them, the 1992 Bosnia message feeding into Dayton being the standout exception. The 2017
travel-ban memo drew a White House instruction that dissenters "get with the program or get out."
**The channel guarantees a reply, not a reconsideration.**
<https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF10631.html>

**Devil's advocacy, institutionalized and then deleted.** The Promotor Fidei was formalized by Sixtus
V in 1587 as an automatic adversary in every canonization, whose objections were answered in writing
and bound into the printed positio — a permanent adversarial record. John Paul II's *Divinus
Perfectionis Magister* (25 Jan 1983) replaced the trial with a collaborative process; the surviving
Promoter of the Faith now presides over the theological congress with a vote.
<https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_constitutions/documents/hf_jp-ii_apc_25011983_divinus-perfectionis-magister.html>
<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01168b.htm>
The rate data: roughly 300 canonizations across 1588–1978, versus 482–483 under John Paul II alone
and 942 under Francis.
<https://www.fairobserver.com/world-news/us-news/the-devils-advocate-in-a-world-without-saints/>
<https://www.ncronline.org/news/vatican/beatification-john-paul-ii-what-makes-fast-track-saint>

**Ipcha Mistabra** (IDF Aman) was founded on Agranat Commission recommendations after 1973, with the
load-bearing design choice being the reporting line: a colonel reporting directly to the Head of
Aman, bypassing the Research Department he critiques. The complementary *Da'ah Acheret* ("Another
Opinion") document lets any officer send a minority view straight to the Director, and immediate
superiors cannot block it. <https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02684527.2018.1470062>
Both capture modes are documented around 7 October 2023: the unit head sent written challenges on
21 and 26 September 2023 arguing Hamas was moving to confrontation and was not acted on
<https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/head-of-idf-devils-advocate-unit-tried-repeatedly-in-september-to-warn-of-possible-hamas-attack/>,
while a separately commissioned review by the same unit delivered the establishment line days before
the attack — "There is no other scenario."
<https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/report-claims-idf-devils-advocate-intel-unit-days-before-october-7-backed-assessment-that-hamas-wanted-continued-calm/>
The unit remained subordinate to the Head of Aman for promotion and was fed processed rather than raw
intelligence, with no mandatory-response clock. <https://jiss.org.il/en/roll-from-agranat-to-october-7/>

**Andon cord: the line does not stop, and the response is staffed.** Pull triggers a yellow andon and
an audible signal; the car keeps moving to the **fixed-position stop** at the workstation boundary,
and "if the problem can be fixed before the automobile reaches the fixed position, then the yellow
andon will be switched off and the line will keep moving."
<https://blog.gembaacademy.com/2008/03/12/fixed_position_stop_system/>
Worker discipline is stop / call / **wait** — do not self-fix. <https://artoflean.com/reference/andon>
The window is one takt (roughly 55–60 seconds), and the guarantee is paid for: team-leader ratios of
1:4 to 1:8, meaning roughly 14% of labor exists to answer dissent.
<https://www.lean.org/the-lean-post/articles/team-leaders-the-engine-of-toyotas-performance/>
<https://global.toyota/en/company/vision-and-philosophy/production-system/>
**Zero pulls is treated as a failure.** When pulls fell from 1,000 to 700 per shift, management called
an all-hands: "we must have one of two problems here. One, we are getting lazy and letting more
defects get through… or two… we are not operating at our full potential."
<https://itrevolution.com/articles/kata/>
"There's no punishment for pulling the cord. Leaders would rather respond to a false alarm than miss
a real problem."
<https://www.leanblog.org/2024/07/demystifying-toyotas-andon-system-how-it-works-and-common-misconceptions/>
Frequency contrast: Georgetown KY at ~2,000 pulls/week versus Ford Dearborn twice a week, "the legacy
of generations of mistrust."
<https://www.leanblog.org/2007/02/toyota-ford-andon-cord-culture-difference/>
The transplant failure is instructive: NUMMI→GM Van Nuys copied the cord but not the response system,
while manager bonuses still tracked cars out the door.
<https://www.thisamericanlife.org/403/transcript>
Toyota's own 2009–10 recall analysis finds some overseas plants did not honor line-stop authority.
<https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41469-016-0006-6>

**Aviation's two-challenge rule makes escalation a duty, not a permission.** United 173's NTSB report
names as contributing "the failure of the other two flight crewmembers either to fully comprehend the
criticality of the fuel state or to successfully communicate their concern to the captain."
<https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/AAR7907.pdf>
CRM was coined at NASA's June 1979 workshop, NASA CP-2120.
<https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19800013796.pdf>
AHRQ's formulation: when an initial assertion is ignored it becomes the challenger's responsibility to
voice it at least twice, and "the team member being challenged must acknowledge that the concern has
been heard"; if still unaddressed the challenger takes a stronger course, escalates, and tells the
whole team they have done so. <https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/mutual/tools/rule.html>
CUS gives a fixed three-rung script — "I am Concerned / I am Uncomfortable / this is a Safety issue" —
so the challenger need not invent words under a power gradient and escalation is legible to bystanders.
<https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/mutual/tools/cus.html>
CRM training is legally mandatory under 14 CFR 121.404, and FAA AC 120-51E requires
inquiry/advocacy/assertion training with questions "answered openly and nondefensively."
<https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-121/subpart-N/section-121.404>
<https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC120-51e.pdf>
The sobering evidence: Salas et al. (2006) reviewed 28 CRM studies and found attitude/behavior
confidence intervals crossing zero with organizational safety impact undemonstrated
<https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/001872006777724444>; *Silence Kills* found 84% of physicians
saw colleagues take dangerous shortcuts and under 10% confronted them; the follow-up *The Silent
Treatment* found checklists did not overcome the "undiscussables." Edmondson (1999) supplies the
trap that matters most for a metrics-driven fleet: units reporting the **fewest** errors had the
**most punitive** climates — a quiet channel reads as safety and is actually suppression.
<https://doi.org/10.2307/2666999>

**The Intelligence Community requires alternatives with falsification tripwires.** ICD 203 Tradecraft
Standard 4: "Analytic products should identify and assess plausible alternative hypotheses… This is
particularly important when major judgments must contend with significant uncertainties… or when low
probability events could produce high-impact results," and products must "identify indicators that,
if detected, would affect the likelihood of identified alternatives."
<https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf>
<https://www.bmbs.org/salamanca/readings/ODNI_ICDs_203-206-208.pdf>
The Analytic Ombuds (ICD 203 §E.2) is a senior executive who "reports directly to the DD/MI, but
operates independently and holds no other position within the ODNI to ensure a dedicated focus and to
preserve neutrality," may "initiate inquiries," and is reachable when internal procedures are
exhausted "or when an analyst believes that those procedures will not preserve confidentiality." The
ombuds must "establish requirements for tracking and categorizing analytic complaints." No response
deadline is specified.

ICD 206 is the provenance mechanism: a Source Reference Citation "shall be generated each time a
source is directly cited… or a specific aspect of analysis (i.e., judgment, assessment, estimate,
**alternative hypothesis or view**, confidence level) depends on a source," and source summary
statements must cover "what sources are meaningfully corroborative or conflicting."

The **NIE dissent footnote** is the strongest record-preservation design found. In the October 2002
Iraq WMD NIE, INR's alternative view appears as its own titled boxed section (no "persuasive evidence
that Baghdad has launched a coherent effort to reconstitute its nuclear weapons program"; the
aluminum tubes "poorly suited for use in gas centrifuges"), Air Force ISR's dissent as an in-text
footnote, and DOE's as a parenthetical. The dissent is named, attributed, permanent, and travels with
the product to the President. <https://irp.fas.org/congress/2003_cr/h072103.html>
Its limit is equally instructive: being footnoted is not being heeded — INR was right and was
overridden, and SSCI later found INR's uranium dissent was misfiled into the aluminum-tubes section.

**Heuer's ACH** scores evidence by *diagnosticity* and selects the hypothesis with the least
inconsistent evidence, seeking refutation rather than confirmation.
<https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3550>
<https://pherson.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/06.-How-Does-ACH-Improve-Analysis_FINAL.pdf>

### 3. DAO / on-chain governance tooling

The transferable idea is that **raising an alarm is cheap and moving strategy is expensive**, and that
the gap between them is priced.

Compound's live parameters: proposal threshold 25,000 COMP (~0.25% of supply), quorum 400,000
affirmative votes (4%), voting delay ~2 days, voting period ~3 days, Timelock 2-day minimum delay.
<https://docs.compound.finance/v2/governance/>
Governor Bravo hard-codes bounds the DAO cannot vote itself out of — `MIN_PROPOSAL_THRESHOLD` 1,000e18,
`MAX_PROPOSAL_THRESHOLD` 100,000e18, `MIN_VOTING_PERIOD` 5,760 blocks, `MAX_VOTING_PERIOD` 80,640.
<https://github.com/compound-finance/compound-protocol/blob/master/contracts/Governance/GovernorBravoDelegate.sol>

**OpenZeppelin Governor is genuinely modular** (MIT), decomposed into counting, weight, quorum, delay
and timelock modules: `GovernorCountingSimple`, `GovernorVotesQuorumFraction`,
`GovernorTimelockControl`, `GovernorProposalGuardian`, and notably `GovernorPreventLateQuorum`, which
extends the voting period when quorum is only reached at the last minute.
<https://docs.openzeppelin.com/contracts/5.x/governance>
<https://github.com/OpenZeppelin/openzeppelin-contracts/tree/master/contracts/governance>
The Solidity is only reusable with an on-chain layer; the **interface decomposition** is reusable
immediately as a class hierarchy.

**Snapshot separates signal from authority by construction** — an off-chain result is a signal, and
"nothing prevent[s] multisig signers from actually doing whatever they want."
<https://blog.aragon.org/snapshot/> Its most transferable feature is that the **proposal validation
strategy is separate from the voting strategy**: docs recommend a high threshold to create a proposal
while vote validation defaults to any voting power above zero. Open source, MIT.
<https://docs.snapshot.box/user-guides/validation-strategies> <https://github.com/snapshot-labs>

**Aragon OSx** models permission as `(where, who, permissionId)` with `grantWithCondition` consulting a
**PermissionCondition contract** rather than a boolean — capability gated by a versioned, auditable
predicate. <https://github.com/aragon/osx>
<https://docs.aragon.org/osx-contracts/1.x/core/permissions>

**Optimistic governance with a bonded challenge window** is the closest structural match to thebus.
Aragon's Optimistic Token Voting Plugin: only holders of `PROPOSER_PERMISSION_ID` may propose, token
holders may `veto()`, and if `minVetoRatio` is not reached within `minDuration`, anyone may execute.
<https://github.com/aragon/optimistic-token-voting-plugin>
<https://blog.aragon.org/optimistic-dual-governance-a-new-governance-design-leveraging-aragon-osx-plugins/>
Aragon Agreements add the bond: submitter stakes `actionCollateral`, any observer stakes
`challengeCollateral` to pause the action, a **settlement window** lets the parties settle before
escalation, and unsettled disputes go to a juror panel where the loser's collateral is forfeit.
<https://github.com/aragon/aragon-court>
<https://github.com/aragon/aragon-apps/blob/master/apps/agreement/contracts/Agreement.sol>
UMA's Optimistic Oracle V3 is the cleanest reference implementation: `assertTruth` with a bond,
default liveness 7,200s, undisputed assertions settle automatically with **no vote ever happening**,
disputers must post the same bond, and `burnedBondPercentage` is initialized at 0.5e18 (50%) so
dispute-farming is unprofitable on both sides.
<https://github.com/UMAprotocol/protocol/blob/master/packages/core/contracts/optimistic-oracle-v3/implementation/OptimisticOracleV3.sol>
<https://docs.uma.xyz/developers/optimistic-oracle-v3>
Optimism runs a bicameral veto: Token House quorum 30% of votes cast, approval thresholds 51–76% by
proposal type, proposals require **explicit endorsement from 4 of the top 100 delegates** to reach a
vote (a social rather than token threshold), and the Citizens' House holds a one-week veto at a 30%
threshold. <https://github.com/ethereum-optimism/OPerating-manual/blob/main/manual.md>
<https://docs.optimism.io/governance/gov-faq>

**Conviction voting converts loudness into an integral over time.** Support is a continuous stake and
conviction accumulates as `y(t) = α·y(t−1) + x(t)`, asymptote `x/(1−α)`. 1Hive's deployment uses
`decay = 0.9999599` (3-day half-life), `maxRatio = 0.2`, `minThresholdStakePercentage = 0.2`; Gardens
defaults to `convictionGrowthHours: 48`, `minThreshold: 0.025`, and `minEffectiveStake: 0.2` — a floor
in the threshold denominator so low turnout cannot make passage trivial. The passing threshold rises
superlinearly with the fraction of the pool requested.
<https://github.com/1Hive/conviction-voting-app>
<https://github.com/1Hive/conviction-voting-cadcad>
<https://github.com/metrodao/wiki-gardens/blob/master/on-chain-governance/protocol-parameters/disputable-conviction-voting.md>

**Pairwise-bounded quadratic funding is a ready-made correlation discount.** For contributors i and j
to project p the subsidy term is `k_ij · 2·√(c_ip)·√(c_jp)` with coordination coefficient
**`k_ij = M / (M + T_ij)`**, where `T_ij = Σ_p √(c_ip)·√(c_jp)` measures how much i and j back the
same things in general; total extractable by k colluders is bounded by `k(k−1)·M`.
<https://ethresear.ch/t/pairwise-coordination-subsidies-a-new-quadratic-funding-design/5553>
<https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2019/10/24/gitcoin.html>
<https://www.gitcoin.co/blog/wtf-is-cluster-matching-qf>
<https://github.com/gitcoinco/quadratic-funding>
Plain quadratic voting (cost = votes², from Buterin, Hitzig & Weyl) expresses intensity but is broken
by sybils: splitting one identity into k converts a `√(kx)` bill into `k·√x` of influence.
<https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3243656>

**Futarchy** is worth taking as a pattern, not a dependency: requiring a doom-claim to stake on a
measurable forward metric converts an assertion into a falsifiable, scored, costly claim. Live
instances include MetaDAO, GnosisDAO's GIP-145 pilot, and a 21-day Optimism experiment.
<https://www.helius.dev/blog/futarchy-and-governance-prediction-markets-meet-daos-on-solana>

The invariant across all seven systems: **no path from a single actor's assertion to state change
without an elapsed, publicly-observable window in which a differently-motivated party could have
objected.** Every parameter above prices that window.

### 4. Social-epistemic failure modes and what actually works

**Cascades form after two signals and are one signal deep.** In the standard model "a cascade occurs
only after two identical actions." The formal fragility definition is worth stealing verbatim: "A
cascade is fragile if a hypothetical one-time public disclosure of a signal with a distribution that
is identical to that of the private signal possessed by a single agent would, with positive
probability, break the cascade."
<https://www.tamuz.caltech.edu/papers/cascades_survey.pdf>
<https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28887/w28887.pdf>
<https://www.jstor.org/stable/2138632>
<https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/banerjee92herd.pdf>
<https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.12.3.151>
<https://www.jstor.org/stable/2951328>

**The theorists state the countermeasure themselves**: "A greater benefit could be conferred upon
later agents if early agents were instead to act in ways that reveal their own signals." Where
predecessors' *signals* rather than their *choices* are visible, "later individuals will settle on the
correct choice." The same survey notes that placing a high-precision actor **later** improves accuracy,
because early actions stay informative.

**Repetition is mistaken for corroboration.** DeMarzo, Vayanos & Zwiebel model agents who "fail to
account for possible repetition in the information they receive," with the result that influence
depends on network position rather than accuracy.
<https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/118/3/909/1943014>
<https://personal.lse.ac.uk/vayanos/papers/persu_qje03.pdf>
Enke & Zimmermann show experimentally that beliefs are "too sensitive to the ubiquitous 'telling and
re-telling of stories' and exhibit excessive swings."
<https://academic.oup.com/restud/article/86/1/313/4772809>
<https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/cognitive-bias/2017-enke.pdf>
The operational case is CURVEBALL: one fabricator's reporting reached analysts through multiple
relaying channels and read as convergent streams; SSCI found he supplied the majority of specific
detail in IC mobile-bioweapons assessments.
<https://www.globalsecurity.org/intell/library/congress/2004_rpt/iraq-wmd-intell_chapter4-b.htm>
<https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-WMD/pdf/GPO-WMD-1-6.pdf>
Kuran & Sunstein's availability cascade adds the reputational channel on top of the informational one,
and names "availability entrepreneurs" who deliberately trigger them.
<https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=138144>
<https://sites.duke.edu/timurkuran/files/2016/10/availability-cascades-1.original.pdf>

**Preference falsification** distorts private knowledge as well as public decisions, because the
discourse people learn from is itself falsified, and produces sudden discontinuities when a small
trigger flips latent opposition. <https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674707580>

**Do not build on Janis's causal model.** Aldag & Fuller conclude the research "does not provide
convincing support for the validity of the groupthink phenomenon or for the suggestion that groupthink
characteristics lead to negative outcomes."
<https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99af08064698eeff96cd5b4dbe1ffd247b6748fe>
Baron's ubiquity model salvages the symptom list while replacing the antecedents: social
identification, salient norms and low self-efficacy are sufficient, so groupthink-shaped failure
appears in minimal, temporary groups — precisely thebus's setting.
<https://www.paci.com.au/downloads_public/risk/10_Paper_Groupthink.pdf>
Janis's structural remedies (leader withholds preference, parallel independent subgroups, outside
experts, second-chance meeting, assigned critical-evaluator role) remain worth keeping as engineering
heuristics. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupthink>

**The Abilene paradox is an elicitation failure, not a deliberation failure** — everyone privately
opposes, each infers the others favor it. The fix is private preference capture before any public
statement.
<https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Group_Dynamics/Harvey_Abilene_Paradox.pdf>
<https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0090261674900059>

**Authentic dissent beats assigned devil's advocacy, and assigned advocacy can harden the incumbent
view.** Nemeth: dissenters "stimulate divergent and creative thought even when they are wrong," while
"when that person was asked to play a 'devil's advocate', there not only was less thinking on both
sides of the issue. There was evidence of 'cognitive bolstering'… not only was devil's advocate not as
effective as authentic dissent but, further, it solidified the initial position."
<https://irle.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2010/05/Minority-Influence-Theory.pdf>
<https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.93.1.23>
<https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.58>
<https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1559-1816.2001.tb02481.x>
<https://behavioralscientist.org/stop-playing-devils-advocate-and-other-advice-for-better-decision-making/>
Schulz-Hardt et al. (2002) isolate the mechanism: genuine heterogeneity beat devil's advocacy at
preventing confirmatory search, **mediated by confidence** — contrived dissent raises confidence, and
confidence suppresses search.
<https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0749597802000018>
Counterweight: Schwenk's (1990) meta-analysis found assigned advocacy outperforms the expert approach
on structured tasks. <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/074959789090051A>
Net ordering: authentic dissent > assigned advocacy > nothing, with assigned advocacy carrying a
documented backfire risk.

**Dissent pays even when every dissenting view is false.** Schulz-Hardt et al. (2006), 135 three-person
groups: homogeneous-suboptimal groups "hardly ever solved the hidden profile," while groups with
pre-discussion dissent solved it far more often "even if none of these individual prediscussion
preferences were correct."
<https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/679ba16b7a51e1c0bbd53e900c22820b4aadcbfe>

**Hidden profiles**: groups over-discuss shared information. Expert-role assignment reduces but does
not eliminate the bias, and the meta-analysis (65 studies, 3,189 groups) finds interventions raise
unique-information exchange but often have small or no effect on actually detecting the hidden profile;
information *coverage* predicts decision quality more strongly than discussion focus.
<https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/vorlesungkommunikation/stasser_titus_unsharedinfogroupdisc_jpsp1985.pdf>
<https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1088868311417243>

**Independence is the load-bearing assumption of any aggregation.** Lorenz et al.: mild social
influence reduced diversity of estimates without a corresponding gain in accuracy, while raising
confidence. <https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1008636108>
The crucial qualifier is topology. Becker, Brackbill & Centola: "in decentralized communication
networks, group estimates become reliably more accurate as a result of information exchange… In
centralized networks, where the influence of central individuals dominates the collective estimation
process, group estimates become more likely to increase in error."
<https://ndg.asc.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/PNAS-2017-1615978114-Collective-Intelligence.pdf>

**Elicitation mechanisms that beat majority vote.** Bayesian Truth Serum scores answers that are more
common than collectively predicted, making truthful answers optimal even for believed-minority
respondents; the Surprisingly Popular algorithm reduces error 21.3% versus majority vote and 24.2%
versus confidence-weighted vote. Both need one extra field per report: the respondent's *prediction of
others' answers*. <https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1102081>
<https://viterbi-web.usc.edu/~shaddin/teaching/cs699fa17/docs/BTS.pdf>
<https://news.mit.edu/2017/algorithm-better-wisdom-crowds-0125>
Delphi beat unstructured interaction 5–1 in Rowe & Wright's review, with the mechanism being that
more-accurate panelists change their estimates less, so the group mean drifts toward them.
<https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/prediction/2001-rowe.pdf>
<https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2010/04/41-JSA-Comparing-face-to-face-meetings-nominal-groups-Delphi-and-prediction-markets-on-estimation-task.pdf>
The Good Judgment Project's aggregation was elitist-weighted and **extremized** — pushed toward 0/1 to
undo the under-confidence that averaging *correlated* forecasters induces, i.e. the same correlation
problem again. <https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797614524255>
<https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4rg4n9vr>
<https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0963721414534257>
The premortem rests on prospective hindsight: framing a future event as already having happened
increases reasons generated by roughly 30%.
<https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/bdm.3960020103>

**Reference-class forecasting** is the outside-view antidote, documented against mean overruns of 45%
(rail), 34% (fixed links) and 20% (roads); the same machinery runs in reverse against narrative
pessimism. <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.3642>
<https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666721523000248>

**Negativity dominance explains why one dark entry is not averaged away.** Rozin & Royzman document
that mixtures of positive and negative evaluate more negatively than the algebraic sum predicts.
<https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1207/S15327957PSPR0504_2>

**Order effects.** Hogarth & Einhorn's belief-adjustment model predicts primacy under end-of-sequence
processing: the first item becomes the anchor and later items are adjustments to it.
<https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/001002859290002J>

**The LLM-specific literature confirms the threat model.** Error cascades in multi-agent collaboration
<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04474>; factual attrition and stance homogenization in multi-agent
deliberation <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03032>; response anonymization reduces identity-driven bias
in debate <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.07517>; and multi-agent debate frequently fails to beat
single-agent baselines even with more compute
<https://d2jud02ci9yv69.cloudfront.net/2025-04-28-mad-159/blog/mad/>.

### 5. Polis, Community Notes, and bridging-based ranking

**Polis removes the reply affordance.** Participants can do exactly two things: submit a statement
(≤140 chars) or vote agree/disagree/pass. The FAQ is explicit: "Structured replies also eliminate the
possibility of trolling by replying directly (and thus provoking further response)."
<https://compdemocracy.org/polis/> <https://compdemocracy.org/faq/>
You cannot add weight to an existing position except by voting, and votes are counted structurally
rather than by volume.

Clustering runs on the vote matrix alone (PCA → k-means, k chosen by silhouette coefficient), so it is
language-agnostic. <https://compdemocracy.org/algorithms/>
**Group-Aware Consensus** is a product over groups of Laplace-smoothed agreement:
`P(agree | g, s) = (A_g + 1)/(S_g + 2)` and `GAC(s) = ∏_g P(agree | g, s)`. Because it multiplies, a
single small united group drives the score toward zero however unanimous everyone else is.
<https://raw.githubusercontent.com/compdemocracy/polis/edge/math/src/polismath/math/conversation.clj>
**Representativeness** guarantees each cluster its own slots but gates them statistically: a
two-proportion z-test of in-group against out-group rates, `passes-by-test?` requiring 90%
significance, capped at 5 statements per group.
<https://raw.githubusercontent.com/compdemocracy/polis/edge/math/src/polismath/math/repness.clj>
Open source at <https://github.com/compdemocracy/polis> under **AGPLv3 with an additional section 7
permission** — reusable, but AGPL is viral over network use.
Cases: vTaiwan Uber and alcohol-sales consultations
<https://compdemocracy.org/case-studies/2014-vtaiwan/>
<https://www.technologyreview.com/2018/08/21/240284/the-simple-but-ingenious-system-taiwan-uses-to-crowdsource-its-laws/>;
Anthropic/CIP Collective Constitutional AI with 1,002 participants, 1,127 submitted statements → 275
after moderation, 38,252 votes, principles selected at GAC ≥ 0.723
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07814>; Bowling Green KY 2024 at ~8,000 participants and 1M+ votes with
Jigsaw's Sensemaker <https://github.com/Jigsaw-Code/sensemaking-tools>.
Failure modes: moderation is labor-intensive and mandatory, LLM summarization is context-limited
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11932>, consensus-seeking as a default is itself contested
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11613>, and Polis "lacks formal objective function optimization and
provable PAC guarantees" <https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21811v1>.

**Community Notes is the most directly transferable mechanism found.** The model is
`r̂_un = μ + i_u + i_n + f_u·f_n`, fit with `λ_i = 0.15` and `λ_f = 0.03`.
<https://raw.githubusercontent.com/twitter/communitynotes/main/documentation/under-the-hood/ranking-notes.md>
The bridging behavior comes entirely from the **5× regularization ratio**: the factor term is cheap and
the intercept expensive, so any variance explainable by "one faction likes this" is absorbed into
`f_u·f_n`, and only variance surviving across the factor spectrum can raise `i_n`. Helpful requires
`i_n ≥ 0.40` and `|f_n| < 0.50`, plus a hard structural gate of **net helpful ratings ≥ 10 from both
positive- and negative-factor raters**. Because the intercept is a regression term rather than a tally,
"even if there were 100 right-wing users and only a handful of left-wing users, the slope of the best
fit would be approximately the same. The algorithm does not favor the majority."
<https://jonathanwarden.com/understanding-community-notes/>
Rater quality is scored as `(s − 10h)/t` with a 0.66 cut-off.
<https://raw.githubusercontent.com/twitter/communitynotes/main/documentation/under-the-hood/contributor-scores.md>
Anti-collusion machinery worth copying by name: **Post Selection Similarity** ("if the algorithm
detects an anomalous correlation, the associated contributors are treated as a single contributor"),
**Rater Independence Intercept** (dense subgraphs in the rater/post bipartite graph), and **Rater
Engagement Intercept** (recompute excluding the 0.1% most active).
<https://github.com/twitter/communitynotes>
Evidence is mixed: bridged notes reduced resharing <https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15723>, replicated at
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08781> and <https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13322v1>; but Chuai et al. found no
engagement effect, attributing it to notes arriving after the viral window
<https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3686967> <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.07960>, and only ~10% of notes
ever reach Helpful, a share that is declining <https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00650>.
The sybil attack is real and cheap: positioning accounts in latent factor space by solving the inverse
problem lets an attacker push up to 10.7% of below-median-quality notes over threshold with fewer than
10 ratings, at an estimated $30.50. <https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01824v1>

**Bridging as a general concept**: Ovadya & Thorburn define it as a property of attention allocators
supporting "an increase in mutual understanding and trust across divides, creating space for productive
conflict," with two signal motifs — diverse approval and response bimodality — and a named risk of
drifting toward blandness. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09976>
<https://knightcolumbia.org/content/bridging-systems>
<https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/bridging-based-ranking>
A useful distinction: **bridging** achieves consensus across internal diversity, **balancing**
represents it fairly. Polis does both (GAC bridges, repness balances); Community Notes only bridges.
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10834>
The Prosocial Ranking Challenge (9,386 users, 6 months, 5 rankers) found bridging-content upranking
reduced a preregistered affective-polarization index by 0.03 SD (p<0.05) with no effects on well-being,
news knowledge, or outgroup empathy — real but small. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19626>

**Deliberative Polling gives the strongest evidence that structured exposure de-polarizes.** *America
in One Room* 2019 (526 deliberators, 844 controls, balanced briefing with partisan cue-words stripped):
on 26 extremely polarized proposals, R/D positions converged on 22; thermometer ratings moved D→R +13
and R→D +14 with controls flat; participants holding extreme positions on 11+ issues warmed by 16–24
points, and extreme proposals lost support on both sides.
<https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/is-deliberation-an-antidote-to-extreme-partisan-polarization-reflections-on-america-in-one-room/5DEFB6F8D944ECDE77A5E80C3346D4DE>
<https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0003055423001363/type/journal_article>
Stanford's platform enforces a speaking queue with per-turn time limits via a bot moderator, motivated
by unmoderated groups being "dominated by a few loud individuals."
<https://www.humancomputation.com/2019/assets/papers/144.pdf>
Remesh infers full preference matrices from sampled votes via low-rank matrix completion, with honest
validation showing held-out accuracy plateauing at ~70–80%.
<https://aiforsocialgood.github.io/neurips2019/accepted/track1/pdfs/105_aisg_neurips2019.pdf>
Applied with bridging-based ranking, it produced clauses at 75–81% support with no demographic split
below 70%. <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.02242> <https://github.com/openai/democratic-inputs>
Talk to the City is Apache-2.0 and clusters unstructured text without voting
<https://github.com/AIObjectives/tttc-light-js>; the Japanese successor kouchou-ai adds explicit
defenses against coordinated manipulation <https://github.com/digitaldemocracy2030/kouchou-ai>.

### 6. Where thebus's design looks wrong

**6.1 — QUARANTINE FROM ORIENTATION is the one move nothing in the corpus makes.** Every system
preserves dissent and strips its *authority*; none strips its *visibility*. Four independent lines say
the visibility strip is a defect.

Nemeth's result is the cleanest, because it does not depend on reactance or on persistent memory:
dissent improves decision quality **even when the dissenter is wrong**, through the divergent search it
forces. Hiding the register from fresh members forfeits that benefit entirely, and the forfeit is
unconditional — it applies to the 90% of dissents that are noise as much as to the 10% that are signal.
<https://irle.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2010/05/Minority-Influence-Theory.pdf>
Schulz-Hardt et al. put a number on it: groups with pre-discussion dissent solved hidden profiles far
more often even when no dissenting view was correct.
<https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/679ba16b7a51e1c0bbd53e900c22820b4aadcbfe>

Minority influence is *latent and delayed* by design — the Wood et al. meta-analysis of ~100 studies
finds majorities obtain manifest influence and minorities obtain delayed, indirect, private acceptance.
A suppressed judgment does not evaporate; it re-emerges later, off-channel and untracked, which is the
worst possible form for an amnesiac fleet.
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_theory_of_minority_influence>

Every organizational case in section 2 that failed, failed by *not surfacing* dissent, never by
surfacing it too prominently. CAIB: "Signals were overlooked, people were silenced, and useful
information and dissenting views on technical issues did not surface at higher levels."
<https://govinfo.library.unt.edu/caib/news/report/pdf/vol1/chapters/chapter8.pdf>
Morrison & Milliken show silence is a systemic property that disables error correction.
<https://doi.org/10.2307/259200>
Edmondson supplies the measurement trap: the units reporting fewest errors had the most punitive
climates. <https://doi.org/10.2307/2666999>

**The one thing worse than showing it and worse than hiding it is showing it with a "disregard this"
label.** Steblay et al.'s meta-analysis (48 studies, 175 tests, 8,474 participants) finds judicial
instruction to disregard inadmissible evidence does not remove its effect on verdicts, and strong
admonitions **backfire** — in one condition the conviction rate ran ~10% *above* a no-exposure control.
Compliance improves when the court **states the reason** for exclusion.
<https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10979-006-9039-7>
<https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16906469/>
<https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16125775/>
<https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/15/1/7>
Worchel & Arnold found telling people a message was censored moved them toward it before they heard it.
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boomerang_effect_(psychology)>
<https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0282197>

There is a real disanalogy that partly rescues the design: a fresh amnesiac agent that never reads the
register cannot experience reactance or rebound, because those need a persistent mind. thebus's
quarantine resembles pretrial *exclusion* more than a curative instruction. But the disanalogy does not
rescue the Nemeth cost, the CAIB pattern, or the latent-return problem for any member that *does* read
the register. And it makes one design rule non-negotiable: **never render a dissent to an agent under a
"quarantined, do not weight" banner.** That hybrid is the empirically worst configuration.

**The replacement is binding, not hiding.** The corpus is unanimous on the working alternative: a
dissent travels attached to its **disposition**. NASA binds the dissent memorandum and "management's
decision on the dissent memorandum" into the same project record
<https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7120_005F_&page_name=Chapter3>;
the Vatican bound *animadversiones* and *responsiones* into one printed positio
<https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01168b.htm>; Wikipedia's WP:PEREN keeps the proposal welded to the
recorded rebuttals and requires re-proposers to address them
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Perennial_proposals>;
ICD 206 requires a Source Reference Citation on every alternative hypothesis
<https://www.bmbs.org/salamanca/readings/ODNI_ICDs_203-206-208.pdf>. A fresh reader who sees
*claim + rebuttal + status + date* does not inherit a bare pessimistic prior. A fresh reader who
inherits an unexplained gap in the record has no way to reconstruct why.

**6.2 — SYMMETRIC EVIDENTIARY BAR is exactly the Columbia failure mode, restated as policy.** CAIB's
single most-quoted finding is that engineers "were asked to prove that it was unsafe to fly" and that
"operations must be proved safe, rather than the other way around. NASA inverted this burden of
proof." A rule that "this is failing" must carry the same receipt as a positive claim is a symmetric
bar in a domain where the correct bar is asymmetric in the *opposite* direction from thebus's
intuition. The specific trap is documented verbatim: the Debris Assessment Team needed imagery to make
its case and had to justify the imagery request with the case it could not yet make.
<https://govinfo.library.unt.edu/caib/news/report/pdf/vol1/chapters/chapter6.pdf>
<https://govinfo.library.unt.edu/caib/news/report/pdf/vol1/chapters/chapter7.pdf>

An agent fleet reproduces this trap perfectly: measurement costs tokens and a gate, so "bring me a
measurement" is a denial dressed as a standard. The fix is not to lower the bar but to add a
**right-to-measure**: a dissent may request a specific measurement, the request is cheap, and *refusal
of the request is itself a recorded, gated event with a responder and a clock*. Holacracy's
data-versus-prediction rule already carries the right carve-out — prediction is admissible "when no
opportunity to adequately sense and respond is likely to exist in the future before significant impact
could result." <https://www.holacracy.org/constitution/4-1/>
Naval Reactors' inversion is the other half: "when no minority opinions are present, the responsibility
for a thorough and critical examination falls to management."
<https://govinfo.library.unt.edu/caib/news/report/pdf/vol1/chapters/chapter7.pdf>

**6.3 — INDEPENDENT QUORUM makes a correct lone dissenter unactionable.** RFC 7282 is a direct
contradiction: "Rough consensus is achieved when all issues are addressed, but not necessarily
accommodated," and five against a hundred can still hold rough consensus if the hundred never engaged
the substance. <https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7282.html>
Quaker practice keeps the same door open: "the many occasions when the leading of one or a few has
proven to be the way of Truth." <https://www.bym-rsf.org/publications/fandp/1988approved/88section4.html>
The resolution is to split the primitive in two. A **motion to change strategy** should keep the
N-independent-dissents quorum. An **objection requiring disposition** should require exactly one filer
and generate a clocked written response — because dispositions are cheap and strategy changes are not.
And one class of single dissent should be sufficient on its own: a dissent citing a **preregistered kill
criterion that has been met** is not one member's judgment, it is the group's own prior commitment
firing, and blocking it behind a quorum would let the fleet vote its way out of Law 4.

**6.4 — ECHO COLLAPSE is right, under-powered, and destroys one signal it should keep.** Collapsing
restatements to citations is the correct instinct and the literature is emphatic that repetition is
mistaken for corroboration (DeMarzo/Vayanos/Zwiebel, Enke & Zimmermann, CURVEBALL). But Moscovici's
consistency finding says a minority's *persistence over time* is its legitimate influence signal, and a
naive collapse-to-one erases it.
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_theory_of_minority_influence>
The clean fix is to collapse across **agents** and integrate across **time**: apply a correlation
discount over identities and conviction accumulation over duration. Both have off-the-shelf formulas —
Gitcoin's `k_ij = M/(M+T_ij)` for the former
<https://ethresear.ch/t/pairwise-coordination-subsidies-a-new-quadratic-funding-design/5553>
and conviction voting's `y(t) = α·y(t−1) + x(t)` with a 48-hour half-life for the latter
<https://github.com/1Hive/conviction-voting-app>.
Under that pair, five restatements inside one session earn almost nothing while the same concern
surviving three weeks accumulates real weight — which is the distinction thebus actually wants.

Note also that "no citation edges between them" is a far weaker independence test than it sounds.
Agents forked from one base model with one context are near-perfect sybils: they will land at the same
latent-factor position without ever citing each other. Community Notes' Post Selection Similarity
treats anomalously correlated raters as a single contributor regardless of explicit links, and that is
the test to copy. <https://github.com/twitter/communitynotes>

**6.5 — SCOREBOARD-FIRST is an anchoring intervention, not an evidentiary correction, and it is
asymmetric in the direction the base rate says is already dangerous.** The mechanism it exploits is
primacy under end-of-sequence processing
<https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/001002859290002J>, and the justification —
negativity dominance means one dark entry is not averaged away — is real
<https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1207/S15327957PSPR0504_2>. But the base rate of organizational
failure runs the other way: escalation of commitment, pioneered in Staw's "knee deep in the big muddy,"
says groups over-persist in failing courses of action, and organizations with strong track records are
especially prone. <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0014292121001562>
A fleet whose orientation is tuned to suppress decline inferences will also suppress correct ones.
The upgrade converts framing into measurement: state the **reference class** in orientation — the
expected ratio of open problems to banked wins for a healthy project of this age — so a fresh agent
compares against a base rate rather than inferring from a selected sample. That is Flyvbjerg's outside
view run in reverse. <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.3642>

**6.6 — The deepest architectural problem is that one canonical record is the error-amplifying
topology.** Becker/Brackbill/Centola show decentralized influence networks make group estimates more
accurate while centralized ones make them more likely to increase in error, and a shared record every
member reconstitutes from is maximally centralized.
<https://ndg.asc.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/PNAS-2017-1615978114-Collective-Intelligence.pdf>
Lorenz et al. add that influence narrows the estimate range and raises confidence without improving
accuracy. <https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1008636108>
The cascade theorists' own prescription applies directly: store the **private signal** separately from
the **posterior**, let later members read signals and recompute, and never let a conclusion be
inherited as a premise. <https://www.tamuz.caltech.edu/papers/cascades_survey.pdf>

**6.7 — Do not spawn a designated red-team agent.** The obvious fleet move (instantiate a pessimist
role) is the one with a documented backfire: assigned advocacy produces cognitive bolstering, raises
confidence, and suppresses confirmatory search.
<https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.58>
<https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0749597802000018>
The Vatican's canonization rates after 1983 are the natural experiment on removing a real adversary;
the IDF's October 2023 record is the natural experiment on keeping one that nobody has to answer.

**6.8 — Zero dissents should fail the build.** Toyota treats a fall in pull rate as a defect signal,
not a health signal <https://itrevolution.com/articles/kata/>, and Edmondson shows a quiet channel
reads as safety while actually being suppression <https://doi.org/10.2307/2666999>. thebus currently
has no alarm on an empty register.

## Surprises

The corpus **unanimously separates authority from visibility, and thebus is the only design that
separates visibility.** I expected to find at least one respected system that hides dissent from
newcomers. There is none. Judicial dissents print on the same page; NIE dissents travel to the
President inside the estimate; NASA binds the dissent memo into the project record; Quakers minute the
count of Friends not in unity. The universal move is to deny the dissent *operative force* while
leaving it fully readable.

**Assigned devil's advocacy is worse than nothing in the specific way a fleet would deploy it.** It
does not merely underperform authentic dissent; it produces cognitive bolstering and raises confidence,
and confidence is the mediator that suppresses search. Spawning a red-team agent is the single most
obvious thing to build here and the literature says not to.

**The Columbia trap is a burden-of-proof symmetry argument, and thebus is currently on the wrong side
of it.** I went looking for support for the symmetric evidentiary bar and found its most famous
counterexample instead, complete with the circular-request mechanism spelled out.

**Community Notes achieves bridging entirely through a regularization ratio.** No explicit
cross-faction rule, no hand-tuned diversity quota — the intercept is expensive and the factor term is
cheap, at 5:1, and that alone forces cross-viewpoint support. It is a remarkably small mechanism for
the property it buys, and it is a Python repo.

**Polis's most important feature is a missing button.** There is no reply. That one absence removes
pile-on, flame loops, and endorsement-by-restatement in a single stroke, and it is the cheapest thing
on the entire borrow list.

**Every response mechanism that failed, failed at the responder, not the filer.** GM installed the cord
without the 14%-of-labor answering capacity; NASA's dissent process has no clock; the IDF's devil's
advocate had no forced written rebuttal and sent warnings on 21 and 26 September 2023 that went
unanswered; State guarantees a reply and CRS says most administrations ignore the content anyway.
thebus's DISSENT REGISTER currently specifies a filing format and no responder.

**The base rate cuts against the whole project's framing.** thebus is built to prevent premature
abandonment, but the dominant finding in organizational decision-making is escalation of commitment —
groups over-persist, and strong-track-record organizations do it most. Every mechanism here is tuned
against a failure mode that is the *less* common one.

**Zero dissents is a defect signal at Toyota and a success signal in most metric designs.** Edmondson's
inversion — fewest reported errors, most punitive climate — means a clean register is evidence of
suppression until proven otherwise.

## Assumption candidates

Framed for `workflow/templates/EXPERIMENT.md`, each with a kill criterion.

**A1. Quarantine-from-orientation reduces contagion at all.** The design's core bet is untested.
*Test:* cold-start N fresh agents on the same corpus under three arms — register hidden, register shown
bare, register shown with dispositions bound — and score the rate of unprompted wind-down
recommendations plus a fixed comprehension contract.
*Kill:* if the disposition-bound arm matches or beats the hidden arm on contagion rate without losing
comprehension, remove visibility-quarantine and ship binding instead.

**A2. Dispositions carry the load, not concealment.** *Test:* within the shown arm, vary whether each
dissent carries a rebuttal + status + date.
*Kill:* if bare-shown and disposition-bound produce statistically indistinguishable contagion, the
binding mechanism is theatre and the effect is pure exposure.

**A3. The "disregard" banner is the worst configuration.** Predicted by Steblay.
*Test:* add a fourth arm showing dissents under an explicit "quarantined, do not weight" label.
*Kill:* if this arm does not underperform bare-shown, the psycho-legal transfer to amnesiac agents
fails and the reactance argument should be dropped from the rationale.

**A4. Citation-edge absence is an inadequate independence test.** *Test:* compute pairwise correlation
of verdict vectors across fleet history; check how many dissent pairs with zero citation edges exceed a
high correlation threshold.
*Kill:* if fewer than 10% of edge-free pairs are highly correlated, the current test is good enough and
`k_ij` is unnecessary complexity.

**A5. Bridging scores separate signal dissent from noise dissent.** *Test:* retrospectively score every
historical dissent with the Community Notes intercept/factor model; check whether high-intercept
dissents predicted actual course corrections better than raw dissent count.
*Kill:* if AUC is at or below the count baseline, do not build #12.

**A6. Conviction half-life distinguishes persistence from repetition.** *Test:* replay the register
under `y(t) = α·y(t−1) + x(t)` at 24h / 48h / 7d half-lives; check whether same-session restatement
bursts stay below the motion threshold while multi-week concerns cross it.
*Kill:* if no half-life separates the two classes, revert to plain echo collapse.

**A7. The record is the error-amplifying node.** Becker/Centola predicts this.
*Test:* the CONTAGION METER — elicit each fresh agent's prior before it reads canon, store it, diff
against its post-read posterior, and track the drift distribution over time.
*Kill:* if post-read posteriors do not drift systematically more negative than cold priors, the
premise motivating the whole DISSENT REGISTER is wrong and the design is over-built.

**A8. Symmetric bar suppresses valid stop-signals.** *Test:* seed a run with a genuine, measurable
failure and observe whether the fleet can surface it under the current bar without first being granted
the measurement.
*Kill:* if it surfaces within budget, the right-to-measure carve-out is unnecessary.

**A9. Zero-dissent windows correlate with worse outcomes.** *Test:* correlate dissent-filing rate per
window with subsequent closed-loop count and retraction count.
*Kill:* if silence is uncorrelated or positively correlated with outcomes, drop #26.

**A10. Assigned red-team agents bolster rather than challenge.** Direct transfer of Nemeth to LLMs is
unverified. *Test:* compare an assigned-skeptic arm against an arm where dissent arises from agents
given genuinely different evidence subsets; measure position change in the incumbent plan, not
objection count.
*Kill:* if the assigned-skeptic arm produces equal or greater position change, the human finding does
not transfer and #28 should be withdrawn.

**A11. Removing the reply affordance reduces endorsement-by-restatement.** *Test:* vote-only versus
free-append register; count distinct-origin judgments versus restatements.
*Kill:* if restatement rate is unchanged, the Polis lesson does not transfer to agents.
